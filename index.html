Worked on the Vehicle Data Processing (VDP) module — the firmware layer sitting between the CAN bus and the rest of the vehicle's software stack. VDP reads raw CAN signals, validates and processes them, and routes the right data to the right teams. Part of a large multi-team project delivering an automated parking assistance system for a TATA Motors production vehicle, covering parallel, perpendicular, empty slot, uphill, and downhill parking within 6 maneuvers.

// day to day

  • Collaborated daily with cross-functional teams to align on signal interface requirements via ASIMA sheets, understand what each team needed from VDP, and keep integration moving across a large distributed codebase.
  • Defined AUTOSAR SWC interfaces in collaboration with systems architects and ported firmware components to new ECU variants, reducing integration effort through reusable BSW abstraction layers.
// signal health monitor I built

Debugging signal issues across a large multi-team system was slow — engineers had to manually trace individual signals through CAN logs to figure out what was broken and why.

I built a live CAN signal monitor inside VDP.c in C. I grouped 7–8 related signals into single diagnostic flags, creating around 15 flags covering ~100 critical signals shared across teams. The monitor read signals live off the CAN bus and raised a flag the moment anything went out of expected range or stopped behaving correctly. Instead of hunting through hundreds of signals, engineers could look at one flag and immediately know which group was broken.

This reduced debugging time by 35–50% depending on fault severity. The diagnostics team adopted it as a standard tool alongside their own workflow.

// debugging & validation
  • Debugging wasn't just at a desk — connected a laptop directly to the vehicle via CAN, sat in the car during parking maneuvers, read live CAN traces in CANoe, flashed updated firmware to the ECU mid-debug cycle, and iterated until behavior matched expectations.
  • Signal issues ranged from data corruption caused by multiple inputs affecting the same signal, to CAN configuration mismatches, to logic that needed fixing in VDP itself. Fixes came from all directions — filtering in VDP, correcting signal configs, or coordinating with the team that owned the upstream source.
  • Resolved 10–14 defects across the project lifecycle spanning ADAS perception, control, and sensor/actuator subsystems. Authored test reports ensuring MISRA-C compliant firmware met AUTOSAR timing constraints.
